Her story

From the classroom
to the mat.

Before Pilates, I spent two decades in education — teaching GCSE and A Level Dance and Performing Arts, and eventually leading departments. I loved it. But somewhere in the middle of all that, I found Pilates. Not as a fitness trend, not for a beach body — but as a genuine practice that changed the way I understood my own body.

I qualified at Level 4 five years ago — the highest level of Pilates certification available in the UK — and I haven't stopped since. I've worked with people recovering from injuries, people who've never exercised in their lives, people in their twenties and people in their seventies. The body's ability to change and adapt never stops surprising me.

My Pilates Lab grew out of frustration. I kept hearing the same thing from people who wanted to try Pilates: too expensive, too intimidating, too hard to fit around a full life. So I built something that removes every one of those barriers. Not a huge platform with hundreds of videos and a pricing structure you need a spreadsheet to understand — just one simple subscription, taught well, priced fairly.

My background in education informs everything I do. I think about how people learn. I think about what someone needs to hear in their first session versus their tenth. I think about the difference between a cue that lands and one that doesn't. That's what I bring to every class — not just knowledge of the exercises, but a genuine interest in helping you understand what your body is doing, and why.

How I teach

Three pillars.

01

Accessible to every body

Pilates isn't for a particular body type or fitness level. I build every session with the least experienced person in mind — because if you can't follow along, it's my failure as a teacher, not yours as a student.

02

Movement with intention

There are no filler reps in my classes. Every movement has a purpose, and I'll tell you what it is. Understanding why you're doing something is the difference between going through the motions and actually building a practice.

03

No selfies. Just results.

You won't find an Instagram feed full of me doing impressive things in expensive activewear. My Pilates Lab is about you and your body — not my brand. I'll quietly make the content as good as I can, and you'll do the work.

The ethos

My Pilates Lab
is not about me.

I mean that genuinely. There are no Instagram stories full of selfies. No endlessly curated self-promotion. No subscription you have to fight your way out of — cancel any time, in a couple of taps. My Pilates Lab exists entirely for the people who use it.

I want to make Pilates accessible — not just in price (though that matters enormously), but in feel. The intimidation of a studio class. The confusion of not knowing if you're doing it right. The guilt of missing a week. I've tried to design every part of this to remove those barriers, not recreate them online.

If you move your body a little more consistently because of something I made — that's the whole point.
